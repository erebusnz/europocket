## Description

The SN74HC74 contains two independent D-type positive-edge-triggered flip-flops, each with asynchronous active-low preset and clear inputs. On the rising edge of the clock, the value on the D input is transferred to the Q output. The high-speed CMOS device operates over a 2 V to 6 V supply range and can drive up to 10 LSTTL loads. Common uses include dividing a clock by 2 or 4 and converting a momentary push-button into a toggle (latching) function -- the latter being the role of U3 in this project.

## Application Notes

- **Momentary-to-toggle (T flip-flop) configuration:** Tie the D input to the inverted output (Q#) so each rising clock edge toggles Q. Debounce and condition the momentary switch through a Schmitt-trigger buffer (the 74AHC1G14, U2, in this design) into CLK. This turns a cheap momentary button into a latching on/off toggle.
- **Power-on reset:** Use the asynchronous active-low CLR (or PRE) with an RC network to force a known output state at power-up. A resistor to VCC and capacitor to ground on the CLR pin holds it low briefly at power-on, guaranteeing Q starts LOW before normal clocking begins.
- **Unused inputs must be tied off:** Never leave any input (D, CLK, PRE, CLR) floating -- doing so causes excess supply current and unpredictable behavior. Tie unused PRE/CLR to VCC (inactive) and unused D/CLK to VCC or GND. If a whole second flip-flop is unused, tie all its inputs to defined levels.
- **Clock edge quality:** These are standard (non-Schmitt) CMOS inputs and require fast, clean, monotonic edges at CLK. A slow or bouncy clock edge can cause multiple triggering or metastability -- always drive CLK from a buffer/Schmitt device, not directly from a switch or slow RC.
- **Decoupling:** Place a 100 nF ceramic capacitor between pin 14 (VCC) and pin 7 (GND), as close to the device as possible, to supply the transient current of the output switching.
