## Description

The 74AHC1G14 is a single 1-input Schmitt-trigger inverter gate with a standard push-pull output, performing the Boolean function Y = NOT A. It operates over a 2.0 V to 5.5 V supply range and delivers +/-8 mA output drive at 5.0 V. The Schmitt-trigger input provides hysteresis that makes the device tolerant of slow input rise and fall times, allowing it to clean up noisy or slowly-changing signals into fast, clean logic transitions. The SE suffix denotes the 5-pin SOT-353 package.

## Application Notes

- **Schmitt-trigger signal conditioning:** The input hysteresis (typically ~1.8 V at VCC = 4.5 V between VT+ and VT-) makes this gate ideal for squaring up slow, noisy, or RC-shaped edges -- for example debouncing a mechanical switch, cleaning up a slow power-on ramp, or conditioning a signal before feeding a standard (non-Schmitt) CMOS clock input. In this project U2 conditions a trigger/clock edge before the SN74HC74 flip-flop (U3) clock input.
- **Unused inputs:** Do not leave the input floating. Any unused Schmitt-trigger input should be tied firmly to VCC or GND to prevent oscillation and excess supply current. (There is only one input here; ensure it is always driven.)
- **Power supply decoupling:** Place a 100 nF ceramic bypass capacitor directly across pins 5 (VCC) and 3 (GND) with short traces. Even a single-gate logic device can inject switching noise onto the rail, and the fast output edges benefit from a low-impedance local supply.
- **Output edge rates and loading:** The push-pull output produces fast edges (tpd ~3 ns at 5 V). Into long traces or reactive loads this can ring; add a small series resistor (e.g. 33-100 ohm) at the output if reflections or overshoot are observed on the driven node.
- **Voltage tolerance:** Inputs and outputs must stay within -0.5 V to VCC + 0.5 V. The device is not 5 V tolerant above VCC; do not drive the input above the supply rail.
